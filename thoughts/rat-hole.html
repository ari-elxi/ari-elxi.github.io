RAT HOLE

You wake up to a mundane sense of impending doom. You turn to your side and turn off your alarm clock. You do not get up. You let your fingers slide across your phone. You consume your daily soup of news freshly brewed with a drone strike, a robbery, and a new holiday recipe. You spend a few seconds convincing yourself of something. You do not get up.

You get up.

There is no premeditation in your actions. Languishly, you place your foot on the ground, your eyes barely grazing over the walls of the 8 meters long, 9.46 meters wide, 3 meters tall cube that you call your room. You walk along the street outside your apartment entrance, turn right at the second intersection, walk straight until the first traffic light, turn left and… There is no need for unnecessary explanations, just like how there is no need for unnecessary thought in your daily routine. You are a machine. A mechanical observer.

Though on this particular day this machine notices something odd. The streets are usually empty in early mornings, but on this particular day you feel the whistling of breezes knock just a bit too hard on your eardrums. Alongside the wind’s sappy lovesong that attracts no one but aged leaves, you hear not the familiarly irritating chatters of your neighbors but something foreign, something more like thousands of tiny people in a standing ovation.

You ignore the irregularities and walk into your most local coffee shop. It makes mediocre coffee, but has the fortune of being located so close to your abode. No one is here today. You walk up to the counter, muttering your order repeatedly: Hi. I’m doing well, what about you? Great! I will take a large Chai latte with whole milk please. Card please. Thank you!

When you finally arrive at the counter after 13 heavy steps, you will discover that the short lady with purple hair who usually bartends has been replaced. In front of the register now is a small, grey, furry creature. It sits on the table, with its long, purplish tail hanging off of the edge. Its black eyes stare into the space behind you.

Squeak squeak.

You freeze. The letters in your well-rehearsed script sprint out of your mouth, which now hangs slightly open but does not make a sound. You turn around slowly and let reality wash over you. Of course! How have you not noticed? On several tables, the same grey creature perched on the edges, each with a comically large-looking cup in front of it. Squeak squeak, squeak squeak. Upon a few seconds of closer inspection, you can now make out a tiny bow on the neck of a rotund creature identical to the one worn daily by the gardener Mrs. Perry, and a particularly upright creature standing post with the same uptightness as the officeman Jim Conway.

Squeaaak! The counter creature pulls your attention back. You mutter a quick sorry and hurriedly hustle out of the door, this time paying special attention to the ground beneath your feet. Of course! There they are, the source of the tiny clapping sounds: thousands of grey furry creatures, sprinting along the road that used to be occupied by cars and buses, their tiny feet tapping quickly against the concrete. Sharp squeaks periodically emerge from the crowd of creatures, resembling all too closely the horns of angry morning commuters.

This is the exact moment you realized your world is over.

Oh god. You legs speed up. Oh god. You focus on one destination. Oh my god. Go home. How can this be? But there is certainly no other way. You kick a creature out of the way and ignore its fists on your shoes. Without a doubt, every human has turned into a rat!

You scroll through questions projected on a blinding screen in your mind as you sit still on your bedsheet. The bedsheets are stained. You have been meaning to wash them for the past two months. How will you live now? You could barely talk to people, but how can you possibly pick up rat language without anyone knowing your language? You can cook, possibly even finally pick up gardening. Surely you can still leave behind paper money in stores in exchange for goods. What if that doesn’t work? What if you have to face the wrath of twenty squeaking creatures and their accusing gazes? Maybe you should just finally take the plunge and kill yourself, like you have been meaning to do for the past ten years of what you call a life. Of course, the plan you had of picking a fight with someone with a gun at a busy bar will not work now; you cannot imagine a rat that produces enough force to fire a shot straight through your temple. Neither can you imagine one rolling a truck through you, turning you into a homogenous flat sheet. Perhaps a knife will do, and perhaps your blood will draw these creatures towards your place until they pile up on your dead body. A 8 meters by 9.46 meters by 3 meters cube made of only rats. You imagine them chewing at your half-lifeless flesh. Would they take your bones home as souvenirs or leave your skeleton lying in its prison? You hope it is the former. You like imagining yourself as a pleasant contribution to many. But as you raise your craft knife to your wrist you still find yourself unable to draw blood. What a pity.

You will then try making a compromise with your cowardice and wither away in thirst or starvation over days, yet you find yourself unable to withstand the growling of your stomach. You convince yourself that it is not the pain but rather the unpleasant sounds that halted your efforts. Eventually, you find yourself again on the streets, unwashed, undead, welcomed by nothing but the tapping of rat paws on concrete. You drag yourself into the nearest grocery store, your fingers clutching most of the cash you have, half of which have been roughly taped together. Squeak squeak? The rat on the counter tilts its head towards you. You flash your basket of frozen food and rat traps in front of its face before placing down all the bills and walking away. Not a single rat followed you out.

Next to your apartment, you set up the glue rat traps. Surely you can catch a few and bribe them to be your translator. You have plenty to offer, given your larger size. Yet somehow, every one of your now-rat neighbors proceed to evade these traps, even those you slide carefully through the cracks of their doors. One morning, you wake up and find your right foot landing on a stack of newspaper in front of your door, sending you to the floor. When you get up again, you find that your left elbow has been perfectly stuck on a god damned sticky rat trap. So this is it.

You pick up the rat traps. Rats scurry past you as you bend down, over and over.

Soon enough, you develop a routine. Each week, you will walk into the grocery store and place down a few crumpled bills in exchange for 7 days worth of frozen food. You will then spend the rest of your time in your cube. Your prison and your safe haven, where the walls are just thick enough to block the squeaking of your neighbors. Sometimes you wonder if the couple next door still engage in daily fights followed by loud, obnoxious sex, but now in the rat form. You wonder if, thanks to the new syntax and nuances of rat language, they can finally discover a new way to see the same problems they have been fighting over for the past year. You wonder how much you have been missing out on, stuck here in boring old English while everyone else enjoys revolutionized entertainment and thoughts enabled by their own new form. You don’t scroll through the news anymore. You don’t watch the same show you rewatched countless times anymore. You don’t go to your university lectures anymore. What use is there? You would not understand a thing anyways. You only get headaches from all the rat noises. If you are not staring at the microwave making its rounds, you are simply there, simply being.

Empty boxes begin to take over a corner of your room. Then a wall. How long would it take for not even your room to have space for you anymore? Posters you have hastily pasted onto the walls, posters of film and music and people and everything and anything that you used to care or pretend you care about, peel off the yellowed paint as they fall. You do not pick them up. You let this room consume you. Maybe only then will you become small like a rat. A rat in a rat society. Dust on your desk. Dust over your clothes on the floor. Dust from the ceiling. Dust lands on your back unnoticed, each particle delivering the incessant insistence of your superior misery. Dust not on rats.

When night falls, you dream no longer of intimate entanglements with vague figures or of exams with questions all too foreign. At some point, the rats must have crawled into your mind and stole the stack of film reels for your nightly screenings. Some of them must have snuck in, carrying reels of flattened, stitched together, and rolled up rats. Your most frequent dream features a near future when the rats finally see you. When the squeaking and tapping on the concrete stop. When they turn towards you at once. When they realize, finally, after all this time, after all this suffering, all this loneliness, that you are different. The broken one who failed to transform. They will then squeak together at once, with such anger that they begin to sound like a real car horn. One time, they began running in circles, piling onto each other with such speed to form a spiraling tornado of rats, charging at you. This is where you open your arms, ready for a final embrace. And this is where you awaken, still oversized for your cube, now one layer smaller as dust accumulated overnight.

You don’t think anymore. You are a machine created in the most introductory course. You perform the action of going to the grocery store. You do not process signals from rats. Sometimes on the street, you feel fleeting thoughts tap on your shoulder, squeaky voices singing behind you: hey, im here! Yet each time you look behind only echoes remain, along with scurrying streams of rats, none looking your way. Why did you even turn around? you can’t remember. You continue.

Your memories now evade you. Coherence becomes a concept all too foreign. You forget your thoughts as they form. Your brain is a highway. A highway filled with rats. Rats speaking in echoes. Echoes of your consciousness. Are you conscious? What is conscious? Can you be conscious if you form and forget your own consciousness continuously at every moment? Squeak squeak. You are on the highway. The rat behind you ushered you on. The light is green. You must head somewhere. Squeak squeak. The flow quickens. Rats climb on top of each other. Spirals of rats. You must move quicker before they —

Squeeeaak. Someone taps angrily at your shoe. You look down and see them flicker their whiskers. Squeak squeak, you say, I’m sorry. You move to the side. And so you are on the street again, searching for something that does not exist.

And so this is how you will continue your life. This is how you will survive for a respectable time, alone in a city turned into rats. You don’t look at the mirror anymore. Sometimes when you catch a glimpse of a puddle you see a pile of rats in a vaguely human form, each rat avoiding your gaze. Sometimes you wake up with a pain in your lower back, as if a long pink tail has been sat on for too long. You don’t have desires anymore. But sometimes you do find yourself muttering something under your breath

pleasepleasepleasepleaseiloveyouiloveyoupleaseohpleaseiwillloveyou

Of course, there is no god you pray to. No one you plead to. Nothing to exert your love upon. You don’t wonder anymore about what you would choose — turning into just another rat in a grey furry river, or retaining your superior intelligence and mobility. Is it better or worse if you become just an oversized rat, too large to fit into a rat-sized slot, too ratty to clutch onto paper bills, too human to forget your misery? You don’t question anymore. You don’t wish anymore. You are, and you always will be.

But this is not where your story ends. You will not forever live as an early stage machine in a city of rats. You will not die an easy death, alone in your cube that can now barely fit your own body, your flesh eaten away by a furry spiraling pile. No.

You wake up to no senses at all. You turn to your side and come face to face with a figure. Something you-sized. Something clothed. Its face and arms are covered in sparse grey hair, with bald spots all over the place. On top of its head perch two small grey ovals. You feel its firm paw on your arm, its fingers wrapping around your wrist, sending a refreshing chill down your veins and into your brain. You tilt your face up to meet its eyes. Its human eyes, with black irises so large that there’s almost no white but a flicker of your own reflection in there.

Squeak squeak, squeak squeak. You say. Good morning, how can I help you?

The creature’s eyes blink. You wait to interpret the squeaks that should follow, only to be met with a language all too foreign, all too familiar.

I see you.

You get up.
